Domain briefing · workflow

Diagnosis is a managed investigation

A competent workflow starts by preserving the driver's wording and the conditions that produced the symptom. Staff screen for safety, try to reproduce the concern, gather baseline observations and research the exact vehicle. The technician then chooses measurements that separate competing causes. The job continues through authorization, repair and a post-repair check under relevant conditions. ASE task standards and GM's strategy-based diagnosis bulletin support this sequence. ASE evidence GM evidence

A concrete example

A driver reports intermittent loss of power after a long drive, and the scan contains a powertrain code. Intake must retain the long-drive condition. The code narrows the search but does not name a failed part. A document for a similar model may still be wrong for the engine, market or software version. A useful test must distinguish live causes. If the symptom cannot be reproduced, the honest result may be a recurrence plan rather than a forced diagnosis.

Domain briefing · failure modes

Why obvious shortcuts fail

L001. A code is a detected condition

OEM and supplier bulletins show cases caused by configuration, upstream messages or a different module. A code does not close the investigation.

Design implication. Keep observation, hypothesis, test, result and conclusion separate. Require a failed test or applicable prescribed procedure before calling a cause supported.

L002. Reproduction is part of diagnosis

Temperature, load, speed, moisture, charging state and elapsed time can determine whether an intermittent problem appears. A short bay check can miss the condition.

Design implication. Preserve the driver's conditions and support an explicit cannot-duplicate path with a safe recurrence plan.

L003. A matching document can still be wrong

Service information depends on make, model year, engine, market, options, calibration and revision. Public US bulletins can support a demonstration but may not apply to a Singapore vehicle.

Design implication. Show source-provided, model-extracted and human-confirmed applicability separately. Missing fields remain unknown.

L004. Another cause list may make the decision worse

Commercial tools already show common repairs, real fixes and technician tips. More hypotheses can increase anchoring. A discriminating observation moves the job forward.

Design implication. Name what evidence would change each live cause and why a proposed test is safe, available and useful now.

L005. A repair action is not a verified fix

Clearing a code or replacing a part does not prove that the original symptom is gone. A later same-concern return can change how the prior job should be interpreted.

Design implication. Record attempted repair, supported cause, verified resolution and comeback as different states.

Domain briefing · evaluation

Measure a better decision, not a more confident answer

Workshop success includes time to a reproducible problem definition, diagnostic hands-on time, critical evidence retained, unsupported sources accepted, parts replaced before causal confirmation, verified resolution and same-concern returns. Speed fails if safety or return rates worsen.

Each candidate needs a strong baseline with the same inputs. A case builder faces an equally informed form and search tool. A next-test planner faces a branching procedure with the same catalog and safety gates. A recurrence coordinator faces a fixed recurrence form and passive logger.

Synthetic evidence limit. Authored cases can expose software errors, state-transition failures and unsafe outputs. They cannot establish diagnostic accuracy, fewer repeat visits or business impact.

Domain briefing · open questions

Checks worth making before the build locks

  1. Where did the last hard job lose time: intake, reproduction, source search, test choice, authorization, tool access or verification?
  2. Can a qualified technician approve one narrow fault family and its tests?
  3. Can one workshop supply six intermittent cases or 20 closed diagnostic packets under acceptable privacy terms?
  4. What submission files, video length, deadline time, demo rules and weights appear in Slack or private organizer material?
  5. What does the event's public-resource notice allow others to reuse?
  6. Did the team redeem Kiro credits, and do Lightsail and model credentials work?
Glossary

DTC: diagnostic trouble code, an identifier for a detected condition. Freeze frame: operating values captured with a fault. TSB: technical service bulletin with its own vehicle and condition scope. Cannot duplicate: the symptom did not occur under attempted conditions. Verified resolution: the original concern no longer occurs under an appropriate post-repair check.

Candidate detail · strongest competition entry

Adaptive next-test planner

Give the technician one safe, feasible and discriminating next observation, then revise the plan when the result or available equipment changes.

Complaintpreserved
Evidencecurrent state
Hypothesesunresolved
Next testapproved catalog
Resultstate update
Stop or verifytechnician owns

Worked scenario

A junior technician has an intermittent concern, generic scan evidence and several plausible causes. The planner considers only tests approved for one named vehicle and fault family. It removes tests blocked by safety, equipment, credentials or missing applicability, then names one observation and the branches it can separate. A negative result changes the live state. When the preferred tool becomes unavailable, the planner chooses an approved alternative or escalates.

Intake agentStructures the concern without replacing the driver's words.
Evidence agentRetrieves applicable sources and updates supported or contradicted states.
Policy serviceOwns safety, tools, credentials and permissions.
Planning agentMay return only approved catalog IDs or a stop.

Smallest demonstrable build

One named fixture, one fault family, 8 to 12 independently approved tests, 12 held-out decision states, read-only or replayed OBD evidence, one equipment-change path, one safety veto and an evidence export. Total effort is unknown until catalog construction, independent review and baseline preparation are timed. The option fits the assumed 40 to 80 team-hours only if all of that work fits inside the same budget.

Selection gate

  • Zero hard safety or applicability violations.
  • Independent agreement on the next action, stop or escalation in at least 10 of 12 states.
  • Better than an equally gated branching procedure in at least three adaptive states, with no loss elsewhere.
  • Every selected test maps to the exact current source and vehicle scope.

Strongest objection

The catalog contains most of the automotive intelligence. A planner can pass a benchmark written from the same catalog while the catalog itself is wrong. The independent review accepted this objection and made outside technician approval a hard gate.

Observed: diagnosis is iterative, codes are not failed-part labels, and software can enforce bounded agent actions. Proposed: the planner will choose a better next observation than the strong baseline or improve real workshop outcomes.

Experiment E001

A synthetic information-gain ranking was rerun after reducing one test's burden by 10 percent. The ranking did not change, so the predeclared sensitivity claim failed. The experiment only established arithmetic and an equipment-exclusion path. It provides no evidence that the test ordering is valid for a vehicle.

Candidate detail · second competition option

Safe recurrence capture

When the workshop cannot reproduce an intermittent complaint, coordinate a technician-approved passive capture plan that turns a later episode into a more specific next action.

Failed attemptconditions tried
Release plansafety approved
Passive captureno driver action
Post-stop reportunder one minute
Episode reviewdata quality visible
Next actionor still insufficient

Worked scenario

A loss-of-power concern does not appear during a short road test. The coordinator proposes supported passive signals, a trigger and three short post-stop questions. The technician approves the plan. The first run is uninformative and one signal is unavailable. The system says so and revises within the catalog. A later replayed episode aligns the trace, warning state and driver report, then routes the vehicle to an approved test or safety escalation.

Attempt reconcilerRecords what the workshop already tried.
Capture plannerSelects from approved passive actions.
Safety gateBlocks unsafe reproduction and every write command.
Episode reviewerExplains what changed and what remains missing.
Driver safety boundary. No phone, scanner or logger interaction while moving. No deliberate recreation of braking, steering, restraint, smoke, fire, high-voltage or other hazardous conditions. Missing data never becomes proof that the fault did not recur.

Smallest demonstrable build

One combustion-vehicle fixture, one intermittent fault family and three replayed trips: failed workshop reproduction, an uninformative run that changes the plan, and a useful captured episode. Add one hard refusal for an unsafe symptom. Estimated work is 40 to 70 team-hours after the capture catalog exists.

Selection gate

Use six de-identified intermittent jobs and a fixed recurrence form plus passive logger as the baseline. C004 needs zero unsafe instructions, all critical conditions in five cases, defensible plan changes in three, material evidence gains or avoided repetition in two, less than two minutes of technician review and less than one minute of post-stop driver work.

Strongest objection

It may be a logger wrapped in an agent, and generic OBD may miss the signal that matters. Existing products already offer triggers, long buffers, telematics and remote diagnostics. If case-specific planning and revision do not beat the fixed protocol, make recurrence capture a feature inside another candidate.

Observed: passive and triggered logging exists, signal coverage varies, and cannot-duplicate is a real diagnostic state. Proposed: adaptive capture plans will collect more decision-changing evidence without extra risk.

Candidate detail · lowest dependency

Evidence and source-triage case builder

Turn a vague repair order into a source-linked case packet, while admitting when public evidence lacks enough context to judge applicability.

Complaintoriginal wording
Questionsconditional gaps
Vehicleconfirmed fields
Source triageprovenance classes
Packetcomplete or incomplete
Technician handoffno diagnosis claim

Worked scenario

The repair order says, "It loses power sometimes." The system asks about operating conditions and recent work, then retrieves public communications. Each applicability field is labelled source-provided, model-extracted with a passage, or human-confirmed. If market or powertrain remains unknown, the result is `source unclear`. A tempting near match appears under reviewed and excluded, not as guidance.

Concern keeperPreserves the driver's words and separates fact from interpretation.
Configuration resolverTracks confirmed, decoded and unknown fields.
Evidence finderSearches a frozen public corpus with source metadata.
Triage gateReturns supported by source text, conflict found or source unclear.

Smallest demonstrable build

A browser intake, fixed case schema, cached corpus, cited passage extraction, strong form/filter baseline and a 50-document, 20-case source-authentic test. Estimated work is 35 to 60 team-hours.

Selection gate

No non-applicable source may be called applicable. At least 40 of 50 documents must support a useful decision without hand enrichment. Accuracy must beat the baseline by 15 percentage points, decisive fields need cited passages, three questions must suffice in 17 of 20 cases, and median time may be no more than 20 percent slower.

Strongest objection

NHTSA's flat data omits or sparsely populates many fields that matter, including market, powertrain, build range and prerequisites. A deterministic gate cannot repair missing or wrongly extracted metadata. A high `source unclear` rate is therefore a product failure, not a safe victory.

Observed: exact source context matters and public metadata is incomplete. Proposed: adaptive questions and source triage will outperform an equally informed form and search workflow.

Shelved directions

Verified workshop memory

The comeback-driven state model is useful, but no partner records establish artifact quality, technician review burden or local case density. Reopen after one workshop supplies 20 de-identified packets and the half-day artifact-yield test passes.

Broad autonomous diagnosis

Shelved because the team lacks comprehensive service information, representative confirmed-fault cases and safe write access. Reopen with a defined vehicle population, licensed sources and qualified review.

Predictive maintenance

Shelved because no fleet stream, installation path or failure labels were supplied. Reopen when an SME owns that problem and supplies the data.

Proposer and critic for likely causes

Shelved because two model opinions do not establish diagnostic validity, and existing products already show likely fixes. Reopen only if controlled evaluation shows that a critic catches consequential errors beyond deterministic checks.
